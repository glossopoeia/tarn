# NNNN. Title (imperative or noun phrase)

- Status: Proposed | Accepted | Superseded by ADR-NNNN
- Date: YYYY-MM-DD
- Covers: design area(s) this decision settles
- Confirm: open sub-questions left for the maintainer, or "none"

## Context

Two to four sentences: the forces at play, grounded in reference-spec precedent
and engineering constraints. What goes wrong if this is left undecided.

## Decision

The decision, in normative-ready language, so spec sections can lift the
phrasing directly.

## Consequences

What becomes easier or harder; what the spec must now define; what is
foreclosed. Include the reader-side obligation this decision creates, not just
the writer-side one.

## Precedent

- Named reference-spec sections or documented history supporting the decision
  (see docs/research/reference-format-lessons.md).

---

Conventions: ADRs are numbered in drafting order, zero-padded to four digits,
never reused. A superseded ADR is never edited in substance — a new ADR
supersedes it, and both link to each other. Spec sections cite the ADRs that
govern them. One page per ADR, hard cap.
