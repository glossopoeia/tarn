## Rejected candidates (suspicions I opened and closed)

1. **My own CRC-32C bug.** My first implementation (`crc32c.py`) zeroed the running CRC's initial value by mistakenly XORing the *parameter* `crc` (default `0xFFFFFFFF`) with `0xFFFFFFFF` before the main loop, which produced wrong answers for every constant in the document (I initially got `0x5f6954b6` where the document claims `0x80A34955`, etc.) and, worse, failed my own self-test (`CRC32C("123456789")` came out `0xa71c05df` instead of the known-correct `0xe3069283`). Because I ran the self-test *before* trusting any spec-derived result, I caught this immediately, fixed the initialization bug (`crc32c_fixed.py`), reconfirmed the self-test passed, and re-ran every computation — all of which then matched the document exactly. ## Findings

### diagrams-01 — AMBIGUITY

**Location.** §20.3, the worked example "(b) Tiles added to an existing layer," lines 3430–3433.

**Quotation.**
```
> before │front│ tiles │ index │ dir₁│pad│tr₁│
> after  │front│ tiles │▓index▓│▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓│ +tiles │ index′ │ dir₂│pad│tr₂│
>                       └ old ─┘                      └ every entry, re-emitted
> ```
```

**Detail.** The annotation line carries two labels under the "after" row. The first, `└ old ─┘`, is fully bracketed and — I verified this by column-indexing both lines character-by-character — aligns exactly under the 8 characters of `▓index▓`, correctly marking the superseded index region as slack. The second label, `└ every entry, re-emitted`, opens with `└` at the column immediately following `+tiles ` (the boundary `│` that separates the new-tiles field from the new-index field) but has **no closing `┘`**, and its text runs to the end of the line. Every other bracketed annotation in this document's diagrams (e.g. the immediately preceding figure (a)'s "untouched" and "was dir₁…tr₁" labels, both closed) is fully closed, so the asymmetry here is not this document's general style.

Two readings are available to a reader:
1. **Narrow reading** — the label describes only the `index′` field (the region that is, in the technical sense the surrounding prose uses, actually "re-emitted": per §20.3 step 4, "Every layer whose stored-tile set or index content this commit changes has its tile-index region re-emitted in full — entry_count entries plus the segment CRC array"). Under this reading the label is simply missing its closing bracket, a typographic slip with no substantive content.
2. **Wide reading** — because the label's left edge sits at the boundary immediately after `+tiles` and no bracket confines it to `index′` alone, and because it runs unclosed to the line's end (which is also where the `+tiles│index′` span visually ends before `│dir₂…`), a reader could take it to describe both `+tiles` and `index′` together as "every entry, re-emitted."

I picked the **narrow reading** as almost certainly the intended one, because the prose is unambiguous elsewhere that newly appended tile payloads (`+tiles`) are *appended*, not *re-emitted* — "re-emit" and "re-emission" are used throughout §20.3/§20.4 specifically for structures whose *bytes already existed* and are being reproduced, possibly relocated (the whole point of §20.4's re-emission rule is byte-identical reproduction of *retained* content); a newly-stored tile has no prior incarnation to re-emit. Labeling `+tiles` as "re-emitted" would be a substantive error, not just a labeling slip, and nothing in the prose supports it. But the diagram's own typography (unclosed bracket, left edge at the `+tiles`/`index′` boundary rather than at `index′`'s own left edge) does not by itself rule out the wide reading, and a reader relying on the figure alone — without cross-referencing the precise verb usage in the surrounding paragraphs — could walk away applying "re-emitted" to the appended tile bytes as well as the index, which would misstate what append-only means for this shape of commit.

**Byte-level?** No. `byte_level_rationale`: this is a labeling/typography ambiguity in a non-normative illustrative diagram; it does not state, and its two readings do not disagree about, any MUST/MUST NOT obligation, any offset, width, or value. Nothing about which bytes a conforming writer may emit or a conforming reader must accept turns on how this bracket is read; the governing normative text (§20.3 step 4, §20.4) is unambiguous on its own and settles the question the diagram's label leaves open. I am reporting it because the charter specifically asks whether a figure could be taken further, or differently, than the prose licenses, and this one technically can be — but I want to be explicit that this is a presentation-quality finding, not a fork in conforming bytes.
