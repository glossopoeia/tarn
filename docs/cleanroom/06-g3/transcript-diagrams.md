## Findings

### CONTRADICTION — §5.1, Example "a file after three commits" (figure, spec/tarn.md L497–502) vs §5.2 [R-CNT-02] and §6.2 [R-CNT-05]/[R-CNT-21]

Byte-level: True

The figure's third under-brace reads "└── the live state ──┘" and, by column alignment, spans exactly three cells: `dir₃` (cols 48–55), `pad` (55–60) and `trail₃` (60–69). §5.2 defines the opposite set: "Coverage is a property of the **live committed state** — the front signature block, and every structure reachable from the live trailer through the live directory's records, extent slots, and tile-index entries, together with the live directory and the live trailer." So the bracket under-covers by three cells — `front`, `layer A`, `layer B` are all live, and the figure's own caption says A's record "still addresses A's original index" — and over-covers by one: §6.2 says of the pre-trailer pad, "Those bytes — the **pre-trailer pad** — are slack (§5.2)", and §5.2 makes slack precisely what the live state does not claim. The figure thus labels as live the one cell that is definitionally not live, and omits every cell that is. Taken further than the section licenses, the picture says a file's live state is its tail.

**Readings.** n/a (contradiction, not ambiguity)

### CONTRADICTION — §20.5, Example "what compaction reclaims" (figure, L2637–2660) vs [R-WRT-07]

Byte-level: True

The caption asserts: "Every live structure survives byte-for-byte and the reference arrays are identical; only placement changes". [R-WRT-07] says the opposite for three structure classes: "A compactor preserves content but not placement … every stored tile's bytes verbatim … and every retained record per §20.4, except that relocation re-points extent slots, regenerates each relocated tile-index region (entry offsets new; entry lengths, entry crcs, and sentinel entries unchanged; segment CRCs recomputed over the entries as re-emitted), and updates the layer-record fields covering a regenerated region (crc_of_crc_array)." The figure reinforces the wrong reading by carrying the label `A: index′` unchanged from the input row to the output row while moving A's tiles between them — every non-sentinel entry offset in that region must change. The figure is also self-refuting: its own annotations go "file_id = X, generation = 7" → "file_id = Y, generation = 1", and the trailer is a live structure, as is the directory whose extent slots [R-WRT-07] re-points. Only the stored-tile extents and the user-metadata extents survive byte-for-byte. An implementer who builds a compactor from this caption emits index regions whose offsets point at the input's layout.

**Readings.** n/a (contradiction, not ambiguity)

### CONTRADICTION — §20.3, Example "the three shapes a commit takes", shape (c) (figure, L2557–2563) vs [R-CNT-25] and [R-LIF-01]

Byte-level: True

Shape (c) is introduced as "**(c) A metadata-only change** — clearing a slab declaration, editing user metadata. Only the directory is rewritten." and the figure shows the commit appending exactly `dir₂│pad│tr₂` with no new extent anywhere. But §7.1 [R-CNT-25] states: "Metadata compression exists only at record granularity: each user-metadata record carries its document in its extent — never inline", and §9.1 [R-LIF-01] states: "Bytes in [0, F) — F being the live trailer's committed file length — are immutable: a writer MUST NOT modify them". An edit of a user-metadata document is therefore a new appended extent (§9.2 step 2: "append … any new record extents (file-scope or layer-scope)"), a re-pointed extent slot, and the old extent turning to slack — three byte effects the figure denies. Only the slab-clearing instance is genuinely directory-only, because S/A_slab live in the layer record's inline payload (§7.4). The figure names two instances and depicts a shape that fits one.

**Readings.** n/a (contradiction, not ambiguity)

### AMBIGUITY — §20.3, Example shape (b) (figure, L2543–2555) vs [R-CNT-21] and §20.3's cost note

Byte-level: False

Shape (b) is titled "**(b) Tiles added to, or superseded in, an existing layer.**" In the "after" row only `▓index▓` and a five-column ▓ run are marked slack; the `tiles` cell is drawn intact, and the new bytes appear as `+tiles`. The caption then states the case the drawing omits: "Superseded payloads become slack too; retained ones do not move." So the figure depicts the additive half of a shape whose title names both halves, and the supersede half exists only in prose.

**Readings.** Reading 1: the `tiles` cell is an aggregate whose superseded subset the figure simply does not resolve — then it omits a case its own title names, and a reader concludes superseding costs no payload slack, contradicting [R-CNT-21] and §20.3's note ("editing one sample of a 10⁶-tile layer appends about 20 MB of index and leaves as much slack"). Reading 2: the figure is the "added" case only, and the supersede case would hatch part of `tiles`. I picked Reading 2 on the caption's authority, but nothing in the figure itself selects it, and the figure is the artifact a skimming implementer takes away.

### AMBIGUITY — §20.3, Example shape (b) (figure, L2546–2550) vs [R-LIF-01] and [R-LIF-02] step 1

Byte-level: False

By column position the "before" row's live tail is `dir₁`[29,35], `pad`[35,39], `tr₁`[39,43]; the "after" row's ▓ run occupies [29,35] only — the old directory alone — and `+tiles` occupies [35,44], i.e. exactly the columns where generation 1's pad and trailer stood. Sibling figures (a) and (c) hatch the whole dir+pad+trailer run ([30,44] against the identical before-row span), so within one example block the same event is drawn two ways. Rows in (b) do share a coordinate frame for `front`, `tiles` and `index`, which invites the positional reading.

**Readings.** Reading 1: "Regions, not offsets" disclaims any cross-row coordinate frame, so the short ▓ run means nothing and the figure is merely inconsistent with its siblings. Reading 2: the frame is real for cells drawn at identical columns, in which case the figure shows a commit placing new tiles over the previous generation's pad and trailer — forbidden by [R-LIF-01] ("a writer MUST NOT modify them") and by [R-LIF-02] step 1 ("begin writing at exactly F"). I picked Reading 1 because of the explicit disclaimer; the defect is that (a) and (c) train the reader to read positionally and (b) then punishes it.

### AMBIGUITY — §9.2, Example "what a crash sees" (figure, L999–1011) vs [R-LIF-04]

Byte-level: False

The legend is "▓ marks the bytes an interrupted commit had written." In row 3 ("committed"), the ▓ run occupies columns [17,37], which is exactly where rows 1 and 2 draw `dirₙ│pad│trailₙ` — i.e. structures a *completed* commit superseded, which are slack under [R-CNT-21], not bytes any interrupted commit wrote. One glyph, two meanings, one legend. Separately, the figure moves from row 2 (torn) to row 3 (committed n+1) with nothing marking the mandatory step between them: [R-LIF-04] says "If that validation fails, the file is torn: the writer MUST NOT append, and locating the last committed state is the deliberate recovery operation of §9.6 — never an implicit step of opening for append." The prose under the figure supplies this; the figure alone reads as "a torn commit is simply overwritten by the next one."

**Readings.** Reading A: ▓ means "bytes outside the live committed state" uniformly, and the legend states only the row-2 instance. Reading B: the legend is literal, making row 3 assert that an interrupted commit wrote dirₙ, its pad and trailₙ — false on the figure's own rows 1–2. I picked Reading A; it is the only one under which the figure is internally consistent, and it is not what the legend says.

### AMBIGUITY — §20.5, Example "what compaction reclaims" (figure, L2640–2646) vs [R-CNT-17], §7.4 and [R-GEO-02]

Byte-level: False

Both rows of the figure carry the cells `A`, `B`, and `A: index′`. Layer B has no index cell in either row, while layer A's index is drawn explicitly — an asymmetry the reader must resolve. §7.4 grants extent (0,0) only to "a layer with zero addressed tiles", and [R-CNT-17] fixes extent_length = entry_count × 20 + ⌈entry_count/2¹⁶⌉ × 4, so any layer with stored tiles necessarily owns a nonempty index region; a zero-addressed-tile layer ([R-GEO-02]) has no stored tiles to draw as `B` either.

**Readings.** Reading 1: `B` abbreviates "B: tiles+index", as §5.1's and §20.3's figures label such a cell explicitly — then the labelling is inconsistent within the document and the reader cannot tell which cells include indexes. Reading 2: B has no index region — a state no conforming file can exhibit while also having a `B` payload cell. I picked Reading 1. The cost of the ambiguity is that the figure is the document's only picture of what a compactor must enumerate, and it enumerates one index for two layers.

### CONTRADICTION — §20.5, Example caption (L2656–2660) vs [R-LIF-04] and [R-LIF-06]

Byte-level: False

The caption claims: "This is also the only operation that shrinks a file." Two other operations shrink one. [R-LIF-04]: "a writer whose physical file is longer than F MUST either truncate to F before step 1 of [R-LIF-02], or overwrite from F and truncate the physical file to the new committed_file_length no later than step 6". [R-LIF-06]: "The nearest-to-end valid candidate is the last committed state; truncating to it recovers the file." What is actually unique to compaction is shrinking the *live committed state* without discarding a generation — which is the property the surrounding paragraph is really about. As written the caption overstates a uniqueness the lifecycle rules do not grant.

**Readings.** n/a (contradiction, not ambiguity)

### AMBIGUITY — §5.1 Example figure (L498–501) vs [R-SLB-05]

Byte-level: False

The figure gives each layer one contiguous cell (`layer A`, `layer B`) with the under-brace `└tiles+index┘`, implying that a layer's stored tiles and its index form one run and that two layers' structures never interleave. §16 [R-SLB-05] grants no such thing: "A layer that declares neither alignment nor a slab makes no layout promise: its stored tiles MAY occupy any non-overlapping byte positions, in any order". §20.5's figure draws the refutation — `│ A │▓▓▓▓│ B │▓▓│ A: index′ │` — but a reader who stops at §5.1 has seen only the grouped form.

**Readings.** Reading 1: the cells are illustrative grouping with no layout claim (supported by the figure's own "Regions, not offsets"). Reading 2: a layer occupies a contiguous region. I picked Reading 1, because §20.5's figure and [R-SLB-05] both exclude Reading 2. Worth flagging because the only contiguity this format ever promises is [R-SLB-03]'s, and only for stored tiles inside one slab run — §19.4's region-read note says so explicitly, and the §5.1 picture pulls the other way.

### AMBIGUITY — §5.1 Example caption (L506–507) and §20.3 shape (c) caption (L2558) vs [R-LIF-01]

Byte-level: False

Both captions use "rewritten" for structures that are appended, never modified: "only the directory, the pad and the trailer are rewritten per commit" (§5.1) and "Only the directory is rewritten" (§20.3(c)). [R-LIF-01] fixes the vocabulary the other way: "A TARN file changes only by **commit**: an append-only transaction that extends the file and never modifies a committed byte."

**Readings.** Reading 1: "rewritten" = re-emitted at a new offset, the previous copy becoming slack. Reading 2: "rewritten" = written over in place. I picked Reading 1: §5.1's very next sentence says "a commit appends and never rewrites a committed byte (§9.1)", and §20.3's example block closes with "In every shape the file grows and no committed byte changes". The two senses of the same word appear two sentences apart in §5.1, which is the whole of the ambiguity — and it compounds with the §20.3(c) contradiction above, where a reader who takes Reading 2 gets an updater that edits a user-metadata extent in place.

### OBSERVATION — Appendix A (hex dump, L3210–3237); §4.5 [R-ENC-09]; §15.4 [R-IDX-05]

Byte-level: False

Appendix A is the document's only complete worked file, and nothing in it can be checked without first implementing CRC-32C — the audit is decidable but front-loads the checksum. I implemented it and the dump verifies completely: 128 bytes exactly ([R-CNT-27]); front block byte-identical to §6.1's constant; directory [16,20) = `00 00 00 00`, the CRC-32C of zero bytes, matching §4.5 and §7.1's rule that the region's final 4 bytes cover the preceding dir_length − 4 = 0 bytes; pad [20,64) zero per [R-CNT-03]; and every [R-CNT-06] gate passes — magic at [64,72), version_major 1, version_minor 0 (the [R-CNT-26] floor), flags 0, generation 1 ≥ 1, dir_offset 16 ≥ 16, dir_length 4 ≥ 4, 16 + 4 = 20 ≤ F − 64 = 64, committed_file_length 128 = the dump's length and a multiple of 64, file_id 0x0123456789ABCDEF ≠ 0, reserved [120,124) zero. CRC-32C over trailer bytes [0,60) computes 0xE00938D2, matching the stored `D2 38 09 E0`. I also reproduced [R-ENC-09]'s check vector (CRC-32C("123456789") = 0xE3069283) and [R-IDX-05]'s all-sentinel full-segment constant 0xEECB3BB2 over 65,536 × 20 bytes. §15.4's worked example is arithmetically consistent too (16 segments, last covering 16,960 entries; entry 999,999 at segment-relative byte 339,180; array at region byte 20,000,000; region 20,000,064; a full segment 65,536 × 20 = 1.25 MiB). The expense is the point: three of the document's numeric constants are unverifiable by inspection, and a reader who cannot execute CRC-32C cannot use Appendix A as the self-check it is offered as.

**Readings.** n/a


## Verdict
The document does not stand alone at its figures. Nine figures exist; three are clean (§8.2's chain of trust, §15.1's index-region schematic — the only normative figure, and correct — and Appendix A's hex dump, which I verified computationally including its CRC), and six carry defects. Three are byte-level: §5.1's \"live state\" bracket names the pre-trailer pad as live and omits the front block and both layers, inverting [R-CNT-02] at both ends; §20.3's shape (c) says a user-metadata edit rewrites only the directory, which [R-CNT-25] and [R-LIF-01] forbid; and §20.5's caption promises that every live structure survives compaction byte-for-byte, which [R-WRT-07] explicitly denies for index regions, layer records, the directory and the trailer — and which the figure's own file_id/generation annotations refute. Three more are non-byte-level ambiguities: §9.2's ▓ legend carries two meanings across its own rows and elides [R-LIF-04]'s mandatory recovery step; §20.3's shape (b) draws only the additive half of a shape its title says covers superseding, and lets new tiles sit at the previous generation's pad and trailer columns; §20.5 omits layer B's mandatory tile-index region and claims a uniqueness ([R-LIF-04]/[R-LIF-06] truncation also shrinks a file) it does not have. Every defective figure sits inside an *Example* block and is therefore non-normative under [R-CNF-01], so no file's conformance predicate moves — but the pictures are the fastest route into the lifecycle chapters, and three of them give an implementer the wrong answer about which bytes move.
