### Reasoning

The finding rests on a premise that the document does not contain: that §20.3's carve-out states the abandoned stride-1 form. It does not. Its words are "a filter whose mandated stride makes it the identity (`[R-COD-10]`)". That is a criterion stated by effect, not by stride value. The abandoned Appendix G form — visible in hunk-07.diff's minus lines — was "§17.3 makes it the identity where the stride is 1"; §20.3 never carried that sentence, so hunk 7 left no restatement of it behind. Both briefs paraphrase §20.3 as naming "only the stride-1 case" (A-05: "attributes filter identity to the stride alone"; B-03: "still names only the stride-1 identity"), but neither can quote §20.3 saying "1", because it does not.

Worse for the finding, §20.3's criterion is not merely non-narrow, it is exact. For a given layer the decoded image length L is fixed by geometry and §14; the only parameter the filter contributes is the mandated stride s. So "whose mandated stride makes it the identity" ranges over exactly the set the repair describes: s = 1 (identity for any L) and s = L (n = L/s = 1). I checked the arithmetic against [R-COD-10]'s own normative sentence — "the filtered image places byte i of group j at offset i · n + j" — and computed the permutation for (L,s) = (2,2), (4,4), (12,4), (8,1): identity for n = 1 and for s = 1, non-identity for s > 1 and n > 1. B-03's brief claim that filter identity is a biconditional on s = 1 or n = 1 is correct; its claim that §20.3's words exclude the n = 1 case is not.

That also disposes of the charter signature the briefs invoke — a corrected property living only in non-normative text. The corrected property is normatively derivable from [R-COD-10]'s permutation formula, which is prose in §17.3, not a blockquote, and therefore normative under [R-CNF-01] ("All text in this document is normative except: §1; any text labeled Note, Example, or Rationale; and appendices identified as non-normative"). §20.3's carve-out cites [R-COD-10] at exactly that point. Appendix G item 5 restates the consequence for a non-normative profile; it is not the property's only home.

Second, even granting the narrow reading arguendo, the same sentence forecloses the harm. It opens by declaring the list illustrative and naming the operative test: "The test is the effect on such a tile, and the list names where that effect arises rather than standing on its own". A flip to filter 2 on an n = 1 layer leaves every superseded-generation stored tile's bytes meaning exactly what they meant, so it passes the stated test and is not barred, whatever one makes of the example.

Third, B-03's byte-level scenario depends entirely on a "closed-carve-out reading" it sources to the shrink paragraph. Its quotation is truncated in a way that reverses the sentence's force. The full sentence reads: "What counts as such an alteration is the bar above and nothing else, its carve-out included — a new padding position whose stored byte already holds the fill `[R-SER-05]` fixes is not one, so a shrink producing only those is permitted here exactly as it is there, and this sentence answers the rescue question rather than restating the test." B-03 quotes up to "is not one ..." and drops the closing clause, which says in terms that the sentence is not a restatement of the test. Read whole, "nothing else" narrows what counts as an alteration in the shrink case and "its carve-out included" imports the carve-out into that case; it does not convert the carve-out into a closed enumeration. Under the reading the document actually supports, updater U2 in B-03's scenario is simply wrong, and the set of files a conforming updater may emit does not move. ### Reasoning

The finding has a real core but both reports overstate it, and X-06's byte-level claim is foreclosed by text neither reporter weighed.

WHAT SURVIVES (the actual defect). [R-CNF-13]'s test is stated over invariants — "reaches ... when, and only when, the invariant it violates is one `[R-CNT-24]` fixes in every format major version" (lines 228-231) — but its complement is stated over structures: "Every other condition this specification names is one of a structure this text defines for format major 1 alone" (245-247). Those two sets do not coincide, and F18 sits in the gap. F18 does not reach: §21.2's preamble derives the reaching set as "F8, F14, F6 and both of F1's instances ... and no other row" (3474-3476), so F18 falls under "every other condition". But its structure is not major-1's. `[R-CNT-24]` (840-847): "In every format major version, permanently: the final 64 bytes of a file contain the trailer magic at F − 64, version_major and version_minor at F − 56 ...". version_major is fixed in every major by a rule, not a row, so §21.1's row-versus-rule precedence cannot rescue the sentence. I searched for a deciding sentence that would make the structural clause true of F18 or exclude F18 from its quantifier and found none: §21.2's carve-out is about the *file* ("a case outside the premise: version_major = 0 declares no major at all"), not about the *condition*, and in the same breath it confirms F18 is not among the reaching rows. So a normative sentence asserts something false of a condition it ranges over. That is an overclaim, and it is the same "state the complement in the wrong currency" slip the repair was elsewhere fixing.

### Constructions tried

A. Multi-major non-resident reader (SUCCEEDED — the byte-level fork). Reader implements majors 1 and 7; remote transport; open path leaves [0,16) non-resident; file declares version_major = 7 with bytes [0,8) byte-reversed. Reading A: MUST apply F6/F1-front, fetch, reject. Reading B: MAY omit, version gate passes, accept and serve ([R-CNF-09] does not bar it because the reader is not obliged to detect). Opposite accept/reject verdicts on identical bytes.

B. Single-major (major-1-only) reader on the same file (FAILED to fork the verdict). Both readings reject, via [R-CNT-07]'s unimplemented-major rejection. Only the diagnostic differs — and here I found a secondary wrinkle the downgrade also missed: [R-CNF-13] says the distinct [R-CNT-07] diagnostic "is owed where every reaching condition is satisfied", which is a fact about the file, not about what the reader checked; a reader that omitted F6 cannot know whether it is owed. That is a diagnostic defect, not a verdict fork, so I did not rest anything on it.

C. Verifier (FAILED, as the reporter conceded). [R-CNF-11] ("checks every file-level requirement — including those ordinary readers are exempted from") and F6's "a verifier holds no such exemption (§2.3)" both remove the exemption from verifiers under either reading. Verdict identical.

D. Writer / [R-CNF-05] escape hatch (FAILED for the downgrade, not for the finding). The project's stated discriminator asks whether the disputed file carries a register-row violation that [R-CNF-05] bars every conforming writer from emitting. Here the hatch is inapplicable rather than satisfied: the file declares major 7, this document defines writers for major 1 only, so no writer of this document emits it under either reading and the emittable set is untouched. The fork is entirely on the reader side, which the discriminating test covers ("must accept or reject").

E. Updater and compactor (FAILED). Updaters inherit every reader obligation ([R-CNF-06], [R-CNF-11]'s "Verifiers and updaters are readers of the files they process"), so an updater forks the same way a reader does and adds nothing new; and no updater of this document processes a major-7 file to a new committed state, so no construction leaves the damaged structure "unstored, de-addressed, superseded, or turned to slack".

F. Precedence convention (FAILED to rescue the downgrade). I searched for any stated specific-over-general rule that would make [R-CNT-04]'s MAY qualify [R-CNF-13]'s MUST: grep for "more specific|specific rule|general rule|takes precedence|prevail|narrower rule|qualifies" returned nothing, and a broader grep for "notwithstanding|conflict|override|supersede|except as|subject to" returned 40 hits, none of them a precedence rule between requirements. The document's only precedence rule is §21.1's row-vs-rule rule, which does not reach this collision.

G. Reading the MUST as pure applicability (leg 2's construction, PARTIALLY SUCCEEDED for the downgrade, not decisively). It is the charitable reading and probably the intended one. But the paragraph reserves "assessed" for applicability in its own closing sentence and uses "a reader MUST apply" here, in parallel with the verifier's duty in the next sentence — so the actor-duty reading has plain textual warrant, and §2.1's phrasing-discipline Note reinforces it.

### Decisive quotes

1. The repair's severing clause, spec/tarn.md lines 227-228 (the object of the MUST, new in hunk 1): "A **condition** this specification names — one malformed state, as §21.1 individuates them, and not the requirement that defines it —". The pre-image's object was "requirement" ("A reader MUST apply every reaching requirement to a file whatever major it declares, and MUST treat its failure as reject-file on that requirement's own ground"), which carried [R-CNT-04]'s MAY automatically. The post-image's object expressly is not the requirement.

2. The MUST and the PP the downgrade relies on, lines 236-241: "A reader MUST apply / every reaching condition to a file whatever major it declares, and / MUST treat its failure as reject-file on the ground of the requirement / stating it rather than on capability grounds". The PP's stated contrast is "capability grounds", it modifies "treat its failure as reject-file", and it is reached only after failure — so it cannot carry an exemption from applying.

3. The unqualified MAY, [R-CNT-04], lines 760-764 (untouched): "A reader that would have to issue a further range request to obtain those bytes MAY defer or omit the check: its open path is anchored at the trailer (§6.2), and the two-request accounting of §6.4 does not include the front block." No qualification by declared major anywhere in [R-CNT-04].

4. Why leg 3's reductio does not transfer — F6's Condition cell, line 3487, qualifies the register's own detector assignment inline, which is exactly what [R-CNF-04] takes as its object: "the obligation binds a reader holding those bytes resident, and a reader that would have to fetch them MAY defer or omit the check (`[R-CNT-04]`)".

5. Why leg 4's register argument cannot resolve a collision with [R-CNF-13] — §21.1, line 3410: "where a row and the rule it cites diverge, **the rule governs**". F6's Governing rule cell (3487) names "`[R-CNT-04]`", not [R-CNF-13].

6. The actor the downgrade did not enumerate — [R-CNF-03], line 160: "A reader MUST accept every conforming file of a format major version it implements".

7. Why acceptance is permitted under the exempting reading — [R-CNF-09], §2.4: "A reader MUST NOT return data as valid from a scope this specification rejects and whose rejection this reader is obliged to detect".

8. Appendix H's supporting text is non-normative — heading at line 4458: "### Appendix H — Conformance checklists (non-normative)", and at 4459-4461: "each entry's authority is the requirement or section cited, never this summary". Its line 4511-4513 ("which `[R-CNT-04]` lets a reader not holding it resident defer or omit and §2.3 does not (F1, F6)") therefore corroborates intent without deciding the text — [R-CNF-01] excludes it.

### Missing sentence

The document nowhere states normatively that a condition's own non-major-based exemptions survive [R-CNF-13]'s reach obligation. [R-CNF-13] is missing a carve-out in the MUST itself, at spec/tarn.md line 237, of the form: "A reader MUST apply every reaching condition to a file whatever major it declares — subject to any exemption the condition's governing requirement grants on a ground other than the declared major, [R-CNT-04]'s residency exemption being the only such exemption in this text — and MUST treat its failure as reject-file on the ground of the requirement stating it rather than on capability grounds."

The only text in the document that currently says this is Appendix H at lines 4511-4513, which is headed "(non-normative)" at line 4458 and disclaims its own authority, so under [R-CNF-01] it carries no force. Every argument the downgrade offers for the exemption surviving — that the MUST is applicability rather than modality, that the "ground of the requirement stating it" clause routes the obligation's terms back to [R-CNT-04], that a general MUST coexists with a specific MAY by convention — is reasoning that appears nowhere in spec/tarn.md as a normative sentence. That is a defect in the document, not a refutation of the finding.
