# ADR-0113 repair audit — third run: refute-phase verdicts

Six byte-level downgrades attacked, on `sonnet`. One overturned.

## A-01 — DOWNGRADE_STANDS
**Bar 2's stated ground reads an "only" into the re-emission rule that is not there, and that "only" forbids the commit bar 2 permits**

**Sections opened:** spec/tarn.md: §3.1 Terms (lines 341-400, esp. 371-376, the stored/sentinel biconditional); §11.1 Geometry and its validity floor including its Rationale block (1584-1627); §11.2 Grid arithmetic / R-GEO-03 (1629-1652); §11.4 Linearization / R-GEO-06 (1672-1720); §14.3/14.4 boundary on decoded-size declarations (2040-2057); §15.2 The sentinel / R-IDX-02 (2139-2160); §19 layer-access validation list naming "the geometry floor" (2925-2949); §20.3 The append procedure in full, both the re-emission rule (3072-3157) and the inserted bars 1 and 2 plus the growth/shrink passages (3157-3270); §21.1 How 

**Reasoning.** The downgrade rests on two independent grounds plus a sub-claim; I tried to break each and could not.

Ground 2 is the decisive one, and it does not need Ground 1 (the "is channel count geometry?" word-game) to be settled at all. Bar 2's own text only ever permits an ordinal to move for "channels the superseded generation stored no tile for" (lines 3213-3215). §3.1's Terms fixes what that means precisely: a tile is stored in a generation *if and only if* that generation's index entry for it is not the sentinel (lines 371-373) — a biconditional, not a rule of thumb. So every entry bar 2 permits to move is, by construction, an entry that *is* the sentinel — and R-IDX-02 fixes the sentinel to one unvarying 20-byte pattern, `FF×16 00×4` (lines 2147-2149), independent of which ordinal it sits at or which channel it nominally belongs to. Consequently the re-emission rule's actual operative test — "every other entry byte-identical at its disk-ordinal position" — is satisfied *trivially* for every entry bar 2 lets move, regardless of whether "position" is read against the old generation's ordinal or the new one's: a sentinel is byte-identical to a sentinel no matter where either sits. I tried to break this three ways (constant-C reorder, a malformed/damaged carried-forward entry, and a segment-CRC-boundary case) and each attempt either reproduced the same triviality or was independently foreclosed by the §3.1 definition itself (a non-canonical entry is, by the biconditional, *stored*, hence frozen by bar 2 and never in the movable set to begin with). This means the "only for a geometry change" gloss the auditor attacks — even taken at face value, even granting the auditor's premise that a channel edit is not a "geometry change" — never actually forces Writer A to refuse anything: the byte-level criterion the gloss is supposedly weaponizing against non-geometry renumbering is satisfied identically under both the narrow and the broad reading, for every case bar 2 in fact permits. There is no reading under which the emittable set moves, because there is no reading under which the criterion the two sides disagree about the *scope* of actually comes out differently on the ground.

Ground 1, independently, also holds for the auditor's own instantiated case. The auditor picked a channel *insertion* (C: 2→3) specifically to be "a channel-declaration edit, not a geometry change" — but §21's row L32 states in terms as direct as the register gets that channel count C is itself "a layer geometry field" on par with rank d and tile extent Tᵢ (line 3740), and §19's validation list independently groups R-GEO-01–03 together as "the geometry floor and its arithmetic" (line 2942), with R-GEO-01 being exactly the rule that treats rank-0, tile-extent-0 and channel-count-0 as one family of validity floor. Under §21.1's own precedence rule ("where a row and the rule it cites diverge, the rule governs," line 3584) there is no actual divergence to resolve here — the row's content matches R-GEO-01's substance exactly, it merely also names the family "geometry," which is corroborating rather than contradicted anywhere else I found (the one place that groups "geometry, layout, and channel types" as three separate declaration categories, line 2051, is about a different quantity — decoded tile *size* — and is not a definition that excludes channel count from "geometry" as such). So the auditor's own worked example is, on the document's own vocabulary, a geometry change, and the parenthetical licenses it directly — the auditor never actually reached the one edit shape (a pure reorder at constant C) that sits outside that vocabulary, and Ground 2 covers that shape anyway.

The sub-claim (the "unswept echo") also checks out: I read both cited passages in full. §11.1's Rationale (lines 1605-1619) explicitly names "a change to a planar layer's channel declarations that moves a stored tile's disk ordinal" as one of the layer's now-three bars — it was updated by this very repair, not left stale. The §20.3 shrink passage (lines 3257-3260) says the same thing in the same words. Neither is the geometry-only fossil the auditor's finding needed it to be.

What the auditor correctly identifies — and what I could not make disappear — is a genuine internal tension in the *prose*: one sentence (3197-3201) asserts the re-emission rule "licenses a renumbering only for a geometry change," while bar 2 two sentences later (3212-3215) explicitly licenses a renumbering whose cause need not be a geometry change on any reading that excludes channel-count edits. That is a real defect in how the repair explains itself. But the discriminating test given to me is not "is this passage well-written" — it is whether resolving the dispute changes what bytes a conforming implementation may emit or must accept. Having tried every construction I could find, including ones designed specifically to attack the two grounds independently, I found none that survives: the underlying byte-level criterion is satisfied identically under both readings for every case actually in dispute. The downgrade's verdict — not byte-level — stands.

**Constructions tried.** 
1. Auditor's own instance — planar layer, N=2, channels (a,b)→insert x before b, C: 2→3 — tested against Ground 1 (is a channel-count change a "geometry change"?). Killed by §21 row L32 (line 3740: "A layer geometry field whose floor is 1 is 0 | rank d; channel count C; tile extent Tᵢ") and §19's list citing "the geometry floor and its arithmetic ([R-GEO-01]-[R-GEO-03])" (line 2942), both of which fold channel count into "geometry" alongside rank and tile extent — so the auditor's own construction (a channel insertion, C changes) is on the document's own terms a change to a geometry field, and the parenthetical's licence covers it directly. This construction does not survive as a counterexample to Ground 1.

2. Pure channel reorder at constant C (the one edit shape the verifier conceded sits outside "geometry change", to test whether Ground 2 — not Ground 1 — still holds there). Traced it through bar 2's own text: bar 2 forbids moving the ordinal of any tile the layer *stores* in the superseded generation, so a reorder that touches any stored channel is barred outright and is not "permitted" in the first place; the only reorders bar 2 actually permits are permutations confined to channels the superseded generation stored no tile for. Every entry that moves under such a permitted reorder is therefore a sentinel entry, and R-IDX-02 fixes the sentinel to one 20-byte pattern (`FF×16 00×4`) independent of position — so "byte-identical at its disk-ordinal position" is satisfied under either the old- or new-generation reading of "position". No divergence found; construction fails.

3. Tried to break Ground 2's premise "every movable entry is the sentinel" using a *malformed* (non-canonical) index entry an appender carries forward under step 2 (lines 3098-3113), reasoning such an entry is not literally the FF-pattern and so might move without being byte-invariant. Killed by §3.1's Terms: "A tile is *stored in a generation* if and only if that generation's tile-index entry for it is not the sentinel" (lines 371-373) — a strict biconditional. A malformed entry is not byte-equal to the canonical sentinel, so by this definition the tile it addresses counts as *stored*, and bar 2 therefore freezes its ordinal — it cannot be among the entries bar 2 permits to move at all. This closes the gap rather than opening one.

4. Tried the segment-CRC-array angle (§15's index region = entries + segment CRC array) for a reading-sensitive byte difference at a 2^16-entry segment boundary. Killed on the numbers: the auditor's own entry_count values (4 and 6, or the general C·N for any construction actually in dispute) never approach 2^16, so there is one segment either way, and in any case both writers — if they proceed at all — place entries via the same R-GEO-06 formula, so the dispute is only about whether emission is licensed, never about what bytes result if it is. No divergence found.

5. Searched §21 for a register row keyed to a bar-2 violation ("disk ordinal", "channel declaration") to see whether one reading makes the *produced file itself* nonconforming and the other does not. Found none — bar 2, like its neighboring §14 bar, is a writer-process obligation without a register row of its own; the document says as much for the analogous shrink case ("this is precisely where the §14 bar has no witness, the live state being clean", lines 3261-3264), consistent with a bar-2 violation misdirecting index lookups semantically while leaving the resulting bytes structurally well-formed either way. This reinforces rather than breaks Ground 2.


## A-02 — DOWNGRADE_STANDS
**The ordinal bar's own gloss is stated in the live state's tense and over-bars a commit the carve-out permits**

**Sections opened:** §3.1 (definitions of "tile"/"addressed tile"/"stored tile", including "unqualified *stored* means stored in the live generation"); §20.3 step 4 in full (the §14 bar at 3132-3157, its tense sentence at 3140-3144, the sample-type sub-rule at 3159-3195, the ordinal bar and its tense declaration at 3197-3235, the shrink rule at 3237-3264); §21.7's preamble (3880-3925, unlabeled and therefore normative per [R-CNF-01]).

**Reasoning.** The auditor's finding needs the bare "stores" at line 3208 ("Either is such a change wherever the layer stores a tile for a channel that moved") to carry the live/post-commit tense, so that a commit which newly stores a tile for a channel whose ordinal moved is barred even when the superseded generation stored nothing there. Reading the actual passage in full, I could not sustain that premise against five separate pieces of text the auditor's excerpt, taken alone, does not have to answer to:

1. The paragraph declares its own tense one sentence before the bar it introduces. Line 3197: "A further effect is barred on the same tense, though on its own ground" — pointing at the §14 bar's tense, stated at 3140-3141 as "the prior generation's." This is not something the reader has to infer; it is asserted immediately upstream of the disputed sentence.

2. Grammatically, "Either is such a change" is anaphoric to the bar just stated at 3202-3204: "a commit MUST NOT change the channel declarations in a way that alters the disk ordinal [R-GEO-06] assigns any tile the layer stores in the generation this commit supersedes." "Such a change" names a change of that kind — one already qualified by "in the generation this commit supersedes." The trailing clause "wherever the layer stores a tile for a channel that moved" is elaborating when that antecedent condition is met, not introducing an unrelated, unqualified test. Reading it as introducing a second, live-tense rule requires detaching "such a change" from what it demonstrably refers to.

3. Five sentences later the passage states, in bold, exactly the test that resolves the auditor's scenario: "The test is the effect on a stored tile, not the shape of the edit: a commit that moves only the ordinals of channels the superseded generation stored no tile for alters nothing this bars" (3213-3215). The auditor's own construction — insert channel x before b, moving only b's ordinals, where the superseded generation stored no tile for b — is a verbatim match for this sentence's own example. A reader who reaches this sentence (five sentences after the one the finding hangs on, still inside the same paragraph) has the exact case decided as permitted, in the document's own words, marked as "the test."

4. The paragraph closes by generalizing to both bars in the plural: "A layer that stores no tile in the generation this commit supersedes MAY be re-declared freely... — the tense is these bars', so a commit does not earn this permission by emptying the layer as it re-declares it" (3222-3225). This directly states that both the §14 bar and the ordinal bar share the prior-generation tense, immediately after the sentence the finding targets.

5. §21.7's preamble (unlabeled prose, hence normative under [R-CNF-01], and outside the disputed paragraph entirely, so not subject to the "same passage, same slip" objection) independently restates the same rule using the same tense: the two companion bars (sample-type and ordinal) "are witnessed not at all where the change leaves untouched everything §14 fixes for a tile the layer stores in the generation the commit supersedes" (3920-3923). A second, independently-drafted passage encodes the identical prior-generation quantifier for this exact bar.

I also checked the auditor's §3.1 argument on its own terms rather than taking the verifier's word for it: §3.1 says unqualified "stored" defaults to the live generation, but within §20.3's own procedural frame, "live" denotes the pre-commit state being evaluated — confirmed by line 3130-3131's "relocating a live index region is compaction's operation... not an appender's," which is plainly the *current, on-disk, pre-commit* region, not a hypothetical post-commit one. At the moment an appender is evaluating whether a candidate commit is barred, the "live" generation is the one about to be superseded — so even the unqualified default, read in its actual procedural context rather than abstractly, lands on the prior generation, not the auditor's post-commit state. The auditor's inference from §3.1's default clause runs backward, as the verifier argued, and I could not find a rereading that rescues it.

On the second alleged instance (3159-3161, "The §14 bar already reaches many changes... any that alters, for a tile the layer stores, what §14 fixes"): this is a named back-reference to the already-and-separately-stated §14 bar (defined with its qualifier at 3132-3134, restated with the qualifier again at 3172-3174 two paragraphs later: "a commit MUST NOT change the declared sample type of any channel of a layer that stores a tile in the generation this commit supersedes"). The auditor concedes this instance has no operative consequence, which is consistent with it being a pointer to an already-fully-qualified rule rather than an independent, differently-scoped one.

I looked for a construction the verifier didn't use — a channel unstored in the superseded generation but reordered without gaining a new stored tile; a case where "moved" itself is ambiguous (it is not: the passage gives an exact index-based definition at 3204-3207); a case turning on §3.1's "live" default outside the procedural frame — and each either collapses into the same anaphoric/tense-declared reading or is expressly and literally pre-decided by the bolded test sentence. I could not produce a scenario where a reader who reads the full paragraph (not just line 3208 in isolation) would durably land on a live-tense reading, given that the very next bolded sentence names and resolves the auditor's exact case as permitted, and given that both the sentence immediately before (3197) and the sentence at the paragraph's close (3224-3225) explicitly assert a shared, prior-generation tense for this bar.

The byte-level test itself confirms this: under either reading of the disputed clause, the document's own bolded, unambiguous test sentence controls the auditor's scenario and permits Writer B's append. There is no sentence anywhere that states the ordinal bar in a live, post-commit tense — every full statement and every back-reference uses the prior-generation qualifier. The emittable set does not move.

**Constructions tried.** (1) Read "Either is such a change wherever the layer stores a tile for a channel that moved" as introducing an independent, live-tense rule detached from "such a change"'s antecedent — killed by the anaphora: "such a change" grammatically inherits the qualifier from the bar it names at 3202-3204, and the paragraph explicitly declares "the same tense" one sentence earlier (3197). (2) Tried to find a scenario where the bolded test at 3213-3215 would NOT resolve the auditor's exact construction — failed; the auditor's own instantiation (insert x before b, b unstored in the superseded generation, only b's ordinal moves) is a verbatim match for the bolded sentence's own worked example. (3) Tried resting on §3.1's "unqualified stored means stored in the live generation" to force a live-tense default — killed by §20.3's own usage of "live" for the pre-commit state (3130-3131, "relocating a live index region... not an appender's"): at the moment of evaluating the bar, "live" denotes the very generation being superseded, so even the default resolves to the prior-generation tense in context. (4) Tried treating the second alleged instance (3159-3161) as adding independent weight — killed by the auditor's own concession that it has no operative consequence, and by its being a named back-reference to a bar (3132-3139, restated 3172-3174) that is fully qualified wherever it is itself stated. (5) Checked whether the §21.7 preamble or the 1602-1627 Rationale block might cut the other way — both instead corroborate the prior-generation tense (§21.7 preamble is unlabeled/normative and states it directly at 3920-3923; the Rationale block, though non-normative, is consistent). No construction survived.

## A-03 — DOWNGRADE_REFUTED
**The repair pins the ordinal a channel edit moves and leaves ungoverned the addressed tiles the same edit drops**

**Sections opened:** Definitions (addressed tile / stored tile, ~line 371); R-CNF-05 (Conforming writer, ~line 199); §11.1 Geometry and its validity floor, including its Rationale (~1584-1628); §11.2 Grid arithmetic (~1629-1654); §20.3 step 4 in full — the §14 bar's general definition, the sample-type bar, the planar-ordinal bar, the growth paragraph, and the shrink paragraph (~3080-3280); §21.7 What no check detects (~3884-3969); register row L21 (line 3729, entry_count vs. geometry-derived addressed-tile count, N interleaved / C·N planar).

**Reasoning.** The verifier's downgrade rests entirely on one claim: that the shrink paragraph's de-addressing gloss states a general principle about the §14 bar's predicate ("dropping a tile out of the addressed set... is not itself such an alteration") that reaches any mechanism dropping a tile from the addressed set, channel-count falls included, because the parenthetical "(§11.2's grid count falls)" is "explanatory apposition naming the mechanism in view, not a restrictive qualifier."

That claim does not survive checking §11.2's own definitions. §11.2 defines "grid count" Gᵢ as a strictly per-dimension quantity, ⌈Eᵢ/Tᵢ⌉, which composes into the spatial-tile count N = ∏Gᵢ. It defines channel count C as an entirely separate quantity, stated in the same breath only to be multiplied against N to produce the planar addressed-tile count C·N. These are two different declared quantities governed by two different kinds of declarations (extents/tiling vs. the channel list), and the document keeps them separate throughout §20.3 step 4: bar 1 (sample type) and bar 2 (planar ordinal) are stated entirely in terms of *channel* declarations, while the growth/shrink paragraph is stated entirely in terms of an *extent* growing or shrinking. So "grid count falls," read against the document's own vocabulary, cannot denote a channel-count fall — it is definitionally an N-side event. Whether the parenthetical is called "restrictive" or merely "illustrative" is beside the point: either way, the sentence it modifies is never extended by any other passage to cover the C-side event, and the phrase used to introduce it structurally excludes that event.

I checked whether some other sentence rescues the generalization. The verifier's argument 3 cites "the obligations this step states below... bind it as they bind any commit" as evidence the whole paragraph, permission included, is meant generally. But that sentence is about the *consequential bookkeeping* — slack (R-CNT-21), entry_count (R-CNT-17), slab (R-SLB-01/04) — being ordinary obligations that apply to a shrink exactly as to any other commit, not novel rules invented for shrinks. It says nothing about whether the *permission itself* (the de-addressing carve-out) generalizes to a different mechanism; it answers "are the downstream bookkeeping rules shrink-specific" (no), not "is the upstream permission mechanism-general" (undecided). The verifier's cited corroboration, §11.1's Rationale, fails for the identical reason on inspection: its own frame is "Of its three bars the one reaching a **shrink or a growth** is the §14 bar" and its closing line is "The other two bars reach neither a shrink nor a growth" — it is scoped to extent shrink/growth exactly as tightly as the passage it is meant to corroborate, and never once mentions channel declarations. Being non-normative under R-CNF-01, it could at most function as evidence of intent, but even as intent-evidence it supports scope-to-extent, not scope-to-any-de-addressing-mechanism.

I then checked whether the gap actually produces divergent bytes, or whether it collapses into unremarkable writer freedom under R-CNF-05's second sentence. It does not collapse: the two readings license different *operations* on identical input (in-place shrink of entry_count vs. mandatory whole-file rewrite under a fresh file_id), not merely different incidental byte choices within an agreed-legal operation. And I checked whether the divergence is masked by conformance checking — i.e., whether one of the two candidate files is simply nonconforming and R-CNF-05 forecloses it under both readings, which per the charter's own discriminating test would make this NOT byte-level. It is not masked: §21.7 states plainly that a violation of exactly this bar, when it concerns a tile the same commit removes from the live state, "is witnessed not at all" — the prior generation's channel declarations are not part of the live state, so no check on the resulting file can tell whether the removal was licensed. That means both Writer A's fresh-file_id rewrite and Writer B's in-place edit pass every §21 register row regardless of which reading is objectively correct, so R-CNF-05's "MUST emit only conforming files" does not visibly bar either output — the two candidate byte sequences are both facially conforming, and only the (unresolved) text decides which one a correct writer was supposed to produce. That is precisely the byte-level shape the charter's test names: the disputed file is conforming (nothing fires against it) and whether an actor is licensed to produce the smaller in-place file, versus being required to fall back to the fresh-rewrite path, is not settled by anything in the document — only by a generalization the verifier supplied and the text does not state.

The concession the verifier offers in its own writeup — that this is "poorly signposted" — undersells the gap: it is not merely unsignposted, the operative term the carve-out uses ("grid count") affirmatively points away from the case in dispute, and the two mechanisms (extent shrink vs. channel removal) are treated as structurally distinct topics everywhere else in this same step. A defect stands where a reader arrives at the exact question — as bar 2's own two sentences about insert/remove/append of a channel show a reader must — and finds only a carve-out that, by the document's own definitions, answers a different question.

**Construction.** Planar layer, channels (a, b), spatial-tile count N = 2, both channels storing tiles in the generation the commit supersedes, entry_count = C·N = 4, index_encoding 1 so extent_length = entry_count×20 + ⌈entry_count/2¹⁶⌉×4 = 84. The commit removes channel b (C: 2 → 1), keeping every one of a's tiles untouched.

Bar 1 (sample type): no channel's declared type changes. Not violated.
Bar 2 (planar ordinal): a's tiles keep ordinals 0,1 (a stays first); the step's own text says appending or removing the *last* channel "re-indexes nothing and moves no ordinal at all." Not violated, by its own terms.

The main §14 bar's applicability to b's own two now-de-addressed tiles is what's disputed. That question is resolved in the text *only* inside the shrink paragraph, and only for one named mechanism: "Dropping a tile out of the addressed set (§11.2's grid count falls) is not itself such an alteration — a tile the new state does not address carries no §14 obligation and its extent becomes slack." §11.2 defines "grid count" Gᵢ as a strictly per-dimension quantity (⌈Eᵢ/Tᵢ⌉) that composes into N = ∏Gᵢ; it defines channel count C as a separate quantity that multiplies N to give the planar addressed-tile count C·N. "Grid count falls" is therefore, by the document's own vocabulary, exclusively an N-side event (an extent shrink) and structurally excludes a C-side event (a channel removal). §11.1's Rationale — the only other place this principle appears — is scoped identically: "Of its three bars the one reaching a shrink or a growth is the §14 bar... a tile dropped out of the addressed set carries no §14 obligation... The other two bars reach neither a shrink nor a growth." §11.1 is titled "Geometry and its validity floor" and never mentions channel declarations. Nowhere in §20.3 step 4, §11.1, §11.2, or §21.7 is the de-addressing carve-out restated for a channel-count fall, and whitespace-normalized greps for every plausible phrasing of that case return zero hits.

Writer A (textually strict): reads the §14 bar's general predicate ("any tile that layer stores in the generation this commit supersedes") as still reaching b's two tiles — their bytes cease to have a decoded size or a sample offset at all, which A reads as an alteration in "how they decode" — and finds no carve-out that names channel removal, only one that names grid-count falls. A refuses the in-place edit and instead performs the fresh-file_id whole-file rewrite the step prescribes wherever the §14 bar reaches a change ("Such a change is a new layer, or a whole-file rewrite under a fresh file_id"). Output: a different file_id, full re-serialization.

Writer B (generalizes the shrink gloss to any addressed-set drop, whatever the mechanism): performs the in-place append. entry_count 4 → 2; extent_length 84 → 2×20+4 = 44 under R-CNT-17 / the index-encoding-1 formula (L22); b's two former extents become slack under R-CNT-21; a's tiles and their ordinals are untouched; same file_id, committed_file_length grows per the ordinary append rule.

Both files pass every §21 register row: L21 checks entry_count against the geometry-derived count and both writers keep that equality by construction; no row anywhere tests a prior generation's channel declarations against the live state, and §21.7 says why in terms: "the prior generation's channel declarations are not part of the live state, which is all §9.7 walks, so no check can know which ordinal a stored tile used to occupy, or which type its channel carried before" — the identical undetectability that already covers bar 1 and bar 2 violations covers a disputed main-bar violation here too, since it concerns exactly the same prior-generation-vs-live-state gap. So if Writer A's reading is the correct one, Writer B's file is nonconforming in a way no verifier can witness — R-CNF-05 does not retroactively legalize it, and Writer B has produced bytes an implementation following the correct reading would not produce. If Writer B's reading is correct, Writer A's refusal to append in place and its consequent fresh-file_id rewrite is over-caution that yields different bytes for an operation the spec actually permits in place. Either way the two produce different byte sequences for the identical caller request "drop channel b, keep a," and which one is the single objectively correct output turns on a question the text answers only for the grid-count mechanism and never touches for the channel-count mechanism. That is genuine byte-level divergence, not writer freedom under R-CNF-05's second sentence (which licenses divergence only within what the rules leave open, not disagreement about whether a rule is satisfied).

**Constructions tried.** 1. Tried to read the shrink-paragraph gloss ("dropping a tile out of the addressed set... is not itself such an alteration") as a general, mechanism-agnostic statement about the §14 bar's predicate, per the verifier's argument 2. Killed by §11.2's own definitions: "grid count" Gᵢ is a per-dimension quantity feeding N; channel count C is separately defined and multiplied into N for the planar addressed-tile count. The parenthetical "(§11.2's grid count falls)" names a term that, by definition, cannot denote C falling. A generalization the text's own vocabulary forecloses is not available to a reader working from the text alone.

2. Tried the verifier's point that "the obligations this step states below... bind it as they bind any commit" shows the whole shrink paragraph's reasoning (including the de-addressing carve-out) is meant generally, not just for shrinks. Checked the referent: that sentence is about the *consequential bookkeeping obligations* (slack under R-CNT-21, entry_count under R-CNT-17, slab under R-SLB-01/04) being ordinary obligations that bind any commit, not novel ones invented for shrinks — it does not say the *permission* (the de-addressing carve-out itself) is general. It answers a different question than the one in dispute, so it cannot rescue the generalization.

3. Tried the corroborating §11.1 Rationale (cited by the verifier as confirming the principle is about unaddressed tiles generally, with "the grid-count parenthetical dropped entirely"). Checked it directly: it is not parenthetical-free of scope — its own opening sentence frames the whole passage as "Of its three bars the one reaching a **shrink or a growth** is the §14 bar," and its closing sentence is "The other two bars reach neither a shrink nor a growth." The passage is scoped to shrink/growth exactly as tightly as the main-text gloss it corroborates; it does not extend the principle to channel-count changes, it just restates it for the same (extent) mechanism in the same (non-normative, per R-CNF-01) register.

4. Tried to find any register row (§21.2-21.6) or any other rule (R-CNT-17, R-CNT-21, R-SLB-01/04, R-LIF-01/02) that independently pins down whether a channel-count fall is barred, in case the shrink-scoped gloss turned out not to matter because some other rule decided it. None of these rules speaks to whether de-addressing itself is permitted; they all fire only downstream of that question already being answered (they govern entry_count, slack, and slab bookkeeping *given* that a shrink or removal is otherwise legal). Killed by absence: no rule anywhere states the predicate for the C-falls case.

5. Tried to find textual support that "channel count" is itself informally called a "grid count" or folded into grid arithmetic elsewhere in the document (which would rescue the generalization). Reread §11.2 in full and grepped for "grid" near "channel"; grid count Gᵢ is defined per spatial dimension only, and every occurrence of "grid" in the document ties to N/Gᵢ, never to C. Killed by the definitions section itself.

6. Checked whether §21.7's undetectability admission might instead defeat the finding (by making a hypothetical bar-violation irrelevant since R-CNF-05 only requires files a verifier can check). Found the opposite: §21.7 explicitly frames writer conformance as binding even where no check witnesses a violation, and R-CNF-05's plain text ("A writer MUST emit only conforming files") is not qualified by detectability. So the undetectability cuts toward supporting the divergence (two different files both pass every check, yet only one reading is meant to be correct), not away from it.

## C-01 — DOWNGRADE_STANDS
**§21.7 says a commit that supersedes every damaged tile "escapes the §14 bar"; §20.3 says it does not**
